## Goal and strategy
Determine Θ(C7), or tighten its lower and upper bounds, in Lean 4 with Mathlib. Θ(C7) is still an open problem. The best known bounds, taken from the attached research note, are 3.2588 ≲ Θ(C7) ≤ ϑ(C7) = 3.3176672…. The target here is a pure-kernel development: no `sorry`, no `native_decide`, axioms limited to `{propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}`.
- **Core:** strong powers of C7 on Mathlib's `cycleGraph 7`, and Fekete's lemma for the capacity.
- **Upper bounds:** a Lovász ϑ certificate that is multiplicative under Kronecker products, a cubic enclosure of ϑ(C7), and a Haemers-rank obstruction.
- **Lower bounds:** a pure-kernel Radix-7 Trie verifier for explicit independent sets, plus a `ValidTuple` / `HeteroLayered` / `PartGadget` auxiliary-upgrading framework.
- **Searches:** C++/Python searches whose results are certified by Lean.
- **Verification:** the `verify/` suite and `scripts/verify_axioms.sh`.

## Dead ends
- **Upper bounds:** Haemers and fractional-Haemers bounds cannot reach ≥3.5 (Bukh–Cox), and product-closed conic PSD tightenings cannot go below ϑ (Bi–Tang / Sason). Details are in `search/upper/UPPER_RESEARCH_REPORT.md`.
- **Lower bounds:** the Polak–Schrijver n=382, k=108 cyclic code is an isolated outlier. Powers K1^m of the weighted gadget give no gain (Θ(K1)=45). Details are in `search/lower/LOWER_RESEARCH_REPORT.md`.
- **Lean performance:** a `Bool` mask encoding was too slow for `AlphaC7Cube`; the fix was 49-bit `Nat` bitmasks with direct `Nat.land`/`Nat.lor`. Also avoid `fin_cases`/`omega` over 49 cases.
- **Verifier hardening:** `verify_axioms.sh` Stage 1 and `verify_witness.py` went through 8–12 verifier iterations of denylist whack-a-mole. The fix was to switch to closed positive whitelists.
